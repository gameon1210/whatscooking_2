# What’s Cooking — intuitive-use test pack

18 October 2026 · Facilitator materials · Hypothetical scenarios for testing, not research personas.

## How to run it

Give the participant the goal and starting context, not the expected path. Ask them to think aloud; do not explain the navigation or rescue a wrong turn immediately. Use disposable household data. Record what they do and say, separating observation from your interpretation. The expected paths below are for the facilitator and describe the intended experience; some controls are currently missing.

For each task, record: completed without help / completed with help / not completed; time from reading the goal to a verified outcome; first action; wrong turns/backtracking; requests for help; whether the participant can explain what was saved; and the 1–7 ease rating. Do not treat automated browser timings as participant performance. This review has no recruited participants and reports no user success rate.

## Three realistic scenarios

| Scenario Name | User Goal | Starting Point | Expected Intuitive Path |
|---|---|---|---|
| First evening at home | You plan meals for a vegetarian household. Riya needs a milk-free plate. Get the family ready and choose a dinner without an AI key. You may leave optional details for later. | Fresh app, no household created. | Understand the purpose → enter minimal household details → set diet/avoidances → add Riya with only essential details → skip optional favourites if desired → see a suitable dinner with required changes visible → plan it → recognise that it is planned, not already eaten. |
| Monday morning in a hurry | It is a school morning. You have two children, a cook, tomatoes and spinach. Decide the boxes and meals, check what must be bought, and hand the plan to the cook. You accidentally mark one ingredient bought. Recover without starting again. | Saved family with two school children; a cook profile; no message sent. | Open the relevant day → review named boxes and meals → change one choice without decoding ranks → review only missing ingredients for the selected period → choose quantities/items → undo the accidental bought state → review essential dietary instructions → reach the handoff action easily. Stop before sending a real message. |
| Plans changed, then you were interrupted | You alone ordered pasta yesterday; the children ate something else. Begin recording it, pause as if a call interrupted you, return and finish. Correct the date if needed, then find what the family actually ate last week. | Saved household; a proposed plan that differs from reality; incomplete historical records. | Log → choose day/meal/dishes → choose who ate and ordered-in status → leave and resume the draft → save once → locate/edit the same record → inspect past dates without confusing suggestions with actual meals → open the private recap from a predictable location. |

## Five questions after each task

- What did you expect the main button to do, and what actually happened?
- Where, if anywhere, did you pause, go back, or look for a different way?
- How easy or difficult was this task, from 1 (very difficult) to 7 (very easy), and what most affected that rating?
- What is saved now—which day, meal, people and status—and what on the screen tells you that?
- If you needed to correct this tomorrow, where would you go first?

## Exploratory checklist

These are test prompts, not a claim that every item passed. Keep the language of the participant’s goal; avoid teaching the interface during the task.

### Finding the next action

- [ ] The first screen explains the benefit before asking for detailed information.
- [ ] Required and optional information is recognisable without attempting a save.
- [ ] Skip or finish-later is reachable on a small screen.
- [ ] Adding a child does not require understanding adult roles or every advanced setting.
- [ ] The main action remains reachable when text wraps, the keyboard opens, or a sheet scrolls.
- [ ] A returning user can find shopping, ordering, history and recap without remembering which home card contains them.
- [ ] Hiding decorative/home sections does not hide the only route to a core task.

### Choosing and planning

- [ ] Suggested, planned, sent, cooked and logged meals look and read differently.
- [ ] Required dietary changes are visible before selecting or handing off a meal.
- [ ] The user can change their mind without permanently hiding or negatively rating a dish.
- [ ] Alternatives can be browsed beyond the first screen/result limit.
- [ ] Reordering a menu is discoverable through visible controls, with a keyboard alternative to gestures.
- [ ] The shown week has a date range; past empty days do not look like remembered meals.
- [ ] Repeated meals across a week are intentional and understandable.
- [ ] A bulk confirmation makes clear how many meals and which dates it commits.
- [ ] Selecting pantry ingredients gives a nearby, understandable next step.

### Shopping and cook handoff

- [ ] Pending ingredient suggestions are not described as “nothing to buy”.
- [ ] The selected shopping period matches the plan being reviewed.
- [ ] The user can include/exclude an ingredient and understand its meal source.
- [ ] Staple and quantity assumptions are visible and correctable.
- [ ] Marking an item bought stays completed for the same need.
- [ ] Bought/edit/remove mistakes have an obvious recovery path.
- [ ] A cook sees the relevant people, boxes and dietary instructions.
- [ ] The primary handoff action does not require scrolling through a full technical-looking message.
- [ ] Opening another app, sending a message and receiving a reply are presented as different states.

### Logging, correction and return visits

- [ ] A meal can be logged without AI and without knowing the exact dish spelling.
- [ ] The dish picker’s Done/Use selection action is distinct from saving the meal.
- [ ] Normal meals can be attributed to the people who actually ate them.
- [ ] A partially completed entry survives an interruption or clearly offers discard.
- [ ] Dates with an omitted year are resolved visibly; a mistake cannot silently become last year.
- [ ] Correcting a meal updates one record and makes the result understandable.
- [ ] A duplicate date/meal prompts the person to replace or keep another entry.
- [ ] Report wording reflects how much was actually logged, rather than implying a complete week.
- [ ] Empty history/recap states explain the next useful action.

### Input and accessibility

- [ ] Focus enters a sheet and returns to its opener when dismissed.
- [ ] Keyboard users can activate choices and dismiss a sheet without a pointer.
- [ ] Icons have names; meaning does not depend only on colour, fading or emoji.
- [ ] Errors identify the field/problem, preserve input and explain how to recover.
- [ ] Success feedback identifies the saved item and is not covered by bottom controls.
- [ ] Long names, larger text and narrow screens keep actions and status understandable.
- [ ] Repeat with a real phone, its keyboard and a device screen reader; browser simulation is not a substitute.
