What's Cooking 2.0 — Functional Specification (FSD)
Sep 30, 2026 · @Utkarsh Bansal
1. Executive summary
What's Cooking 2.0 moves from a meal log with suggestions to a household food chief-of-staff. It decides the next meal, hands it to whoever cooks, and closes the loop on groceries, leftovers and family feedback.
The April 2026 specs got the core right: it helps you decide what to cook; it is not a recipe app. What they missed is that in Indian homes the decision rarely ends with the planner. It passes to a cook or helper, to a grocery top-up, to a tiffin box that comes back half-eaten, and to a grandparent who fasts on Tuesdays. Version 2.0 is built around that whole chain.
Four bets carry the product:
Decide in 10 seconds, from what you have. Start from the kitchen: tap the vegetables at hand, reuse a leftover, or choose to order in. One "Today card" per meal slot, shaped by context chips (rainy, guests, low energy, fasting day) and hard per-member rules.
Delegate without friction. The Planner pre-approves a prioritised shortlist (first choice, second, backup). It goes to the cook as a WhatsApp "Cook Card" in her language, with a voice note and quantities for the headcount. She picks based on what's actually in the kitchen.
Close the loop. Leftovers become planned next meals, tiffin returns become feedback, missing ingredients become a Blinkit order. On a tired evening, a Zomato order is a valid choice too.
Learn from patterns, as a family. Day, time, occasion, weather, who's eating, and what the family did with leftovers before all shape suggestions. Shared family account, quick votes and occasion memory (what we served which guests).
The product ships on Android and iOS and stays explainable. It adds a shared family account, Indic voice and WhatsApp as a second front end, because that is where the cook, the family and the grocery run already are. Every service is free except the LLM.

The planner-to-cook handoff. No meal app treats the domestic cook as a user. A WhatsApp Cook Card in her language, with quantities and a voice note, is a wedge that global apps have no reason to build.
Mixed-rule households. Jain grandparent, a teenager who eats eggs, Tuesday no-meat, Navratri fasting. Per-member, per-day rules plus a "one base, many plates" split meal.
Occasion memory. Guests, festivals, birthdays, exam weeks: "what did we serve the Sharmas last Diwali?" This is the emotional, high-stress moment competitors ignore.
Lunchbox feedback loop. "Tiffin came back full" is the most honest preference signal a family produces. Nobody captures it.
Leftover chains. Plan the second life of today's dal (dal paratha tomorrow's tiffin) instead of tracking pantry stock.
Plan-to-cart. A missing-items list handed to Blinkit (or Zepto, Instamart) in one tap, and Zomato as a first-class "order in tonight" choice.
Set aside for now: calorie tracking, recipe authoring, meal-kit commerce and restaurant ordering. Each pulls the product toward a crowded category and away from the decision.

5. Product principles
Decisions, not recipes. Every screen ends in a decision (cook this, send this, buy this). Recipes are linked out, never authored.
Zero-install for everyone but the Planner. Cook, elders and kids take part through WhatsApp links and polls.
Rules are hard, preferences are soft. Fasting, allergy and religious rules filter candidates and are never overridden by score. Tastes only rank.
Every suggestion explains itself. One plain reason per card ("Not made in 12 days · Aarav liked it · uses yesterday's rice").
Learn from what happened, not what people say. Accepted plans, tiffin returns and cooked logs outweigh ratings.
Speak the kitchen's language. Voice in and out in Indic languages and Hinglish; dish names as the family says them.
Gentle balance, no diet culture. Weekly nudges based on ICMR-NIN plate guidance; no calorie counts, no shaming.
The user controls the dial. A Familiar-to-Adventurous slider and a pause-learning switch; household data is exportable and deletable.

7. Key user flows
The 9 pm Tomorrow plan is the flow that matters most; the others hang off it.
F1 · Tomorrow plan to Cook Card (target: under 15 seconds for the Planner)
At 9 pm the Planner gets a push: "Tomorrow: Poha · Aarav's tiffins: fruit chaat (short break) + paneer roll (lunch) · Rajma chawal". Rules for tomorrow (for example Dadi's Ekadashi) are already applied.
The Planner taps Confirm all, or swipes any slot to its alternate.
The system saves planned meals, adds missing ingredients to the cart list, and schedules the Cook Card.
For lunch the Planner keeps a ranked shortlist (rajma chawal, then chole, then dal-rice as backup). Confirm opens WhatsApp with the Cook Card pre-filled in Kannada, with a voice note and quantities for 5; the Planner taps Send.
Next morning the cook opens the Cook page link and taps the first choice she can make (no rajma soaked, so chole), or Missing item: tomato. "Missing" pings the Shopper with a one-tap Blinkit handoff.
After each slot ends, meals auto-log. The 9 pm digest next day asks only about exceptions.
F2 · Right-now decision
The Planner opens the app; the Today card shows lunch.
The Planner taps chips such as Low energy and Use leftovers; the card re-ranks to "Lemon rice from last night's rice".
Swipe right accepts. If a cook is scheduled for this slot, the Cook Card goes now.
F3 · Tiffin loop
The tiffin is planned the evening before (F1).
At the pick-up time set per child, a push asks "Aarav's tiffin came back?" Empty, Half, Full.
"Full" twice for the same dish lowers its tiffin score for that child and asks one optional question: Didn't like it, No time, Too much.
F4 · Occasion
The Planner creates "Saturday dinner, Sharma family, 9 people"; the guest group carries rules (one Jain guest).
The system proposes a 5-course menu that avoids dishes served to this group before, and marks the Jain-safe plates.
The Planner edits and confirms; the cart list and a prep timeline for the cook are generated.
Afterwards, one tap records "Served"; the occasion goes into memory.
F5 · Voice log for a missed day
The Planner holds the mic: "kal raat khichdi aur papad, aaj subah idli".
The parser returns two entries (Dinner yesterday, Breakfast today) and the Planner taps Save.
8. Data model and recommendation engine
The engine stays a transparent rules-plus-score ranker. The LLM translates in and out (voice, photos, ask box, reasons, Cook Card text) and never picks the dish itself.
Pipeline
Resolve context: slot, date, who is eating, active day rules, life mode, chips, weather, cook on duty.
Hard filter: remove any dish that breaks a rule for an eating member, unless a per-member variant exists (FR-213).
Candidates: household history, regional catalogue, leftover-chain targets, cook repertoire; limited by the Adventurous dial.
Score: weighted sum (below); weights live in remote config.
Diversify: the top 3 must differ in base (rice, roti, other) and main food group.
Explain: a template reason from the top 2–3 drivers; the LLM may reword it.

How learning works. H and D come from simple counts over the family's own history, grouped by weekday, slot, occasion type and leftover. They decay with a 60-day half-life so recent habits win. 9. Architecture, integrations and non-functional requirements
2.0 is Kotlin end to end: one Kotlin Multiplatform app for Android and iOS, and a small Ktor service on top of Supabase. One language is simplest for an LLM to write and maintain, and the recommender code is shared between phones and server. Every service is free-tier or on-device except the LLM.

10. Release plan, metrics, risks and open questions
There are five phases over about 28 weeks. Each phase opens only when the one before it passes its gate. Phase 2 carries the differentiating wedge (the Cook Card).

release plan · 5 phases, 4 gates, weeks from kick-off
Weeks are relative to kick-off and assume LLM-driven coding with a human reviewer. Gates are measured on a dogfood group of 10–15 families (team and friends) on every build. There is no concierge pre-test: the full experience is built through Phase 3, then a closed beta of 50–100 families (North and South Indian, with and without a cook) runs end to end to find every issue before public launch.
