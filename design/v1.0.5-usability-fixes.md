# What’s Cooking 1.0.5 — usability repair

Closes UX01–UX17 from the 18 October expert walkthrough: 7 high and 10 medium findings.

- Meal records now carry explicit diners; logging and corrections preserve plate identity. Single-person logs do not close whole-family plans. Tiffin attribution follows the selected child.
- Device-local, family-scoped log drafts survive refresh, protect unfinished work when switching meals, and expose storage failures.
- Shopping purchases fulfil specific planned ingredient needs. Archiving keeps fulfilment; undo and later requirements work independently. Shopping carries the chosen planning period and supports ingredient review and quantities.
- Past weeks show records and saved plans only. Future weeks generate sequentially, retain pinned choices and offer a review before bulk pinning.
- Required mild preparation is visible before selection. Cook payloads carry per-plate allergy and exclusion instructions, including alternatives. Common allergen names have short translations in all five supported cook languages.
- Quick child setup, fixed sheet actions, a compact cook handoff, complete dish browsing, named preferences, explicit inferred years, permanent kitchen tools and richer order-in choice reduce avoidable effort.

No store migration is required: new fields are optional or top-level persisted drafts with defaults. Existing family IDs, meal IDs and preferences remain in place. Hidden bought items remain stored so fulfilment and undo survive.

Validation and exact release identity: [repair report](../artifacts/ux-review-2026-10-18/Fixes.md), [test results](../artifacts/ux-review-2026-10-18/fix-test-results.json), [release record](../artifacts/ux-review-2026-10-18/release.json).

This closes the reviewed interaction defects. It does not implement the deferred family sync/invites/sign-in plan, validate every recipe’s allergen data, or certify physical Android/iPhone behaviour. Translations and recipe metadata still need knowledgeable human review before launch.
